# The lenses

The lenses are the tools for seeing the big picture. A technical team sees features; the analysis must show the game: the category, the moat, the channels, the timing. A full analysis uses all eleven. Each lens below has its guiding questions and the document where its answer usually lands.

## 1. The need and the answers to it

- What need does the customer have, in their words?
- How many different answers to that need exist? For example: do it by hand, hire someone, a general tool, a specialized tool, a platform, a service.
- Who sits in each answer, and what price does the customer pay for each: money, time, risk, privacy, lock-in?
- Where do you sit, and are you alone there?

Lands in: 05 and 07.

## 2. What they own and what they integrate

- Split the product into its parts. For software that acts for a user, three parts are worth naming: the brain (models, agents, logic), the hands (drivers, integrations, the machine it acts on) and the distribution (channels, community).
- Which parts do they own, and which do they integrate from others?
- What they integrate is a dependency and a weak point: a supplier's price, terms or roadmap can move under them.
- What they own is their moat, if it is hard to copy.

Lands in: 02, 03 and 05.

## 3. The moat: durable or copyable

- Copyable in weeks: a feature, a technical trick, a UI pattern, a price cut.
- Hard to copy: an architecture the other side refuses on principle, depth in a domain, trust earned over time, a community, distribution deals, data.
- Apply it to them and to you. For each of your leads, say how long it lasts and what would end it.

Lands in: 00, 05 and 07.

## 4. Licenses as an operating boundary

- What can you take with the right notices?
- What can you take only as an idea, rebuilt in a clean room?
- What can you never take?
- Could their license change, and what happens to you if it does?

Lands in: 04 and 06. Details in [licensing.md](licensing.md).

## 5. Real numbers and shop-window numbers

- What do stars, downloads and declared users really measure?
- What are the honest numbers: new installs, running copies, active contributors, paying customers?
- Which of their numbers are claims, and which are measurements?

Lands in: 01 and 00. Details in [numbers.md](numbers.md).

## 6. The business

- The price list, and how they really earn.
- Estimated economics: always inference, with the calculation and the source of every list price used.
- The company: who owns it, who funds it, who owns the marks and the IP.
- What their investors need from them next (inference), and how that shapes their moves.

Lands in: 01 and 07.

## 7. Trust

- Is telemetry on or off by default? Before or after consent? Tied to an identity?
- What leaves the user's machine, and to whom?
- Does the privacy policy say what the code does?
- How hardened is the app: signing, update verification, entitlements, local servers?

Lands in: 02 and 05. Details in [trust.md](trust.md).

## 8. Engineering

- Size and growth, process, tests, cadence, debt.
- How much of the code agents write, and who really holds the project: the bus factor.
- Can they move fast without breaking things, and what is the evidence?

Lands in: 02. Details in [code.md](code.md).

## 9. The relationship

- To you, are they a competitor, a supplier, a channel or a partner? Often several at once.
- List the ways to coexist, from the cheapest: ignore, interoperate, integrate, partner, compete head on.
- What would each side gain and risk?

Lands in: 07.

## 10. "If they..." scenarios

- For example: they update a pinned component, adopt your open code, build what they are missing, fix a defect you rely on, change their license, raise prices, get acquired.
- For each scenario: how likely (inference), what changes for you, and your move.
- Never build your positioning on a defect of theirs: defects get fixed.

Lands in: 05 and 07.

## 11. The mirror

- Of what you say about your product, what is true in the build that ships?
- What is not true yet, and what needs proof before you say it?
- What do they have that you lack? Write it.

Lands in: 00 and 05. Details in [mirror.md](mirror.md).
